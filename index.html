Every community map marker
was a guess. Now we find out.

Leonida's community maps were traced by hand from trailers and screenshots. On launch day the game ships and every one of those markers becomes checkable against the real thing. This desk records which ones survived. Confirmed, corrected, or debunked — with the evidence, per marker.

The gap nobody is filling

There are more than twenty GTA 6 map sites in the wild, and almost all of them published their marker set before the game existed. When the game arrives, those markers are either right or wrong — and no one is keeping score. Verification is unglamorous work that produces the one thing the whole ecosystem lacks: a record of its own accuracy.

What we check

Each marker in the register gets one verdict, one piece of evidence, and where it was wrong, a measured position error in metres against the community's own coordinate system.

What it produces

A per-marker correction record — publishable, licensable, and useful to the map sites themselves, because a corrected marker is a map that stops embarrassing its readers.

Confirmed

Marker is where and what the community said it was.

Corrected

Real, but the position or description was wrong. Fix recorded.

Debunked

No such place exists in the shipping game.

Inconclusive

Could not reach or could not tell. Not scored as a failure.

Work with the desk

This is a measurement, not an opinion. If your product reaches GTA players, that is the audience this desk serves — and the numbers are ones we can prove.

What we can show you

Median minutes from discovery to a verified post. Debunk share, which proves we filter rather than repeat. Number of discoverers credited. Corrections issued, publicly.

What we do not do

No leaks or datamined material. No audience data sold. No uncredited sourcing. Sponsor slots are labelled as such, always.
