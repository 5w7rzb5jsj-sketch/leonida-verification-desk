The gap

Twenty map sites. Nobody keeping score.

Every community map of Leonida was drawn before the game existed. When it arrives those markers are either right or wrong, and no one is publishing which. Verification is slow, unglamorous work — and it produces the one thing this ecosystem has never had: a record of its own accuracy.

Confirmed

Real, and where the community said it was.

Corrected

Real, but misplaced or misdescribed. We publish the fix and the distance.

Debunked

No such place exists in the shipping game.

Inconclusive

Could not reach it, or could not tell. Not scored as a failure.

One verdict, one piece of evidence

Every marker in the register is checked in-game and gets a single label plus the observation behind it. No hedging, no "possibly", no vibes.

A record, not an opinion

The output is machine-readable: which markers were right, which were wrong, and by how many metres. Useful to the map sites themselves — a corrected marker is a map that stops misleading its readers.

Work with the desk

Reach players who are actively looking

This is a measurement, not a content mill. If your product reaches GTA players, this is an audience mid-search — and the numbers we quote are ones we can prove from the log.

  • Median minutes from a discovery to a published verdict
  • Debunk share — evidence we filter rather than repeat
  • Number of discoverers credited by name
  • Corrections issued publicly, including our own mistakes
